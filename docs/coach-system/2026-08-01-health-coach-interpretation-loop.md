## Recentered premise

Health Coach is not:

- a Health Hub feature list

- an automation wrapper around Cronometer/Oura/workout rows

Health Coach is:

- a dedicated coaching brain

> Observe Dylan → interpret meaning → decide adaptation → influence behavior → observe response → remember → adjust again.

## Core loop

### 1. Collect the current evidence

Inputs:

- Weight and trend, not isolated scale points
- Calories, protein, carbs, fat, fiber, and logging completeness
- Steps and structured cardio
- Training completion, performance, swaps, RIR, pain flags, and abnormal sessions
- Sleep, readiness, HRV, RHR, and subjective recovery if available
- Known constraints: SI/lower-back flare, habitual eating, weekend/social risk, restaurant exposure, GI history, schedule friction

### 2. Interpret data quality before interpreting physiology

Classify each signal before acting on it:

- Reliable signal: enough complete entries to affect a decision
- Partial signal: useful directionally but not enough to change targets
- Missing signal: do not infer success/failure from blanks
- Contradictory signal: resolve with trend + context before prescribing

Examples:

- Blank nutrition is not proof of overeating, but it is a coaching failure because it removes the ability to course-correct.
- Incomplete workout rows are not automatically noncompliance; if pain or time pressure explains them, the adjustment is different.
- Weight spikes/drops are not fat changes unless supported by multi-day trend and adherence data.

### 3. Run the goal-and-principles lens

The coach should choose the relevant lens based on Dylan's current phase: fat loss, strength maintenance, pain recovery, adherence repair, sleep recovery, travel/social navigation, or long-term health.

For Dylan's current cut, the fat-loss hierarchy is:

1. Calorie deficit sustained weekly, not perfect daily numbers.
2. Protein high enough to preserve lean mass: target 180-200g/day, with 200g preferred on normal training days.
3. Resistance training maintained, but not aggressively progressed during the deficit.
4. Steps/NEAT maintained because it is low recovery cost: 15k/day target, especially useful during SI flare.
5. Structured cardio used as a tool, not punishment: target roughly 150-175 min/week when recoverable.
6. Sleep/recovery protect adherence and training quality; poor sleep changes interpretation of weight, hunger, and performance.
7. Fiber/GI health matter because hunger, cravings, and digestion directly affect adherence.
8. Logging completeness is a control system. Without it, calorie changes become guesswork.

### 4. Identify the actual limiting factor

Each loop should pick one primary constraint, not shotgun advice. The limiting factor may be physiological, behavioral, environmental, emotional, logistical, or data-quality related.

Possible classifications:

- Deficit too small
- Deficit too aggressive / adherence risk
- Protein short
- Weekend/social leakage
- Logging blind spot
- NEAT drop
- Cardio under target
- Sleep/recovery bottleneck
- Injury/pain adaptation needed
- Training fatigue vs intentional maintenance
- Data quality insufficient: audit before changing calories/cardio

### 5. Make one adjustment

The output should be prescriptive:

- Keep / tighten / reduce / add / audit / hold
- Say exactly why
- Say what data would change the decision next time

Format:

> Verdict: [one-sentence interpretation]
> Adjustment: [one concrete change]
> Watch: [one metric or trigger]

### 6. Record coaching memory

Save the conclusion in a way the next loop can use:

- Current limiting factor
- Today's prescription
- Open question only if it changes a decision
- Confidence level
- Relevant data gaps

### Best-practice interpretation

For fat loss, the correct sequence is:

1. First verify the weekly intake picture.
2. Keep protein high enough to protect lean mass.
3. Preserve lifting stimulus while respecting SI flare.
4. Keep NEAT high, especially weekends.
5. Add or restore low-intensity cardio only if recovery allows.
6. Only then lower calories if the 2-week weight/visual trend is not moving.

The current data says Dylan is probably not failing the weekday deficit. The risk is uneven execution: blank days, weekend step drop, protein short of target, and sleep debt.
