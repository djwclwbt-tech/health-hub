**How the air fryer works (Ninja DualZone):** two baskets that run on their own. **Smart Finish** starts the longer food first, so both baskets finish at the same moment. That's protein in one basket and potatoes in the other. **Match Cook** copies one basket's settings to the other, for a double batch. Most models have Air Fry, Roast, Bake, Reheat and Dehydrate, and some add Max Crisp or Broil. I'll confirm yours from the model number.

**How the oven works:** Convection Bake blows hot air, so food browns faster. Set it 25°F lower than a recipe says, or check early. Plain Bake is better for eggs and anything that should stay soft.

## What a chef project needs that we don't have yet

These are the usual pieces of an AI kitchen system. Each one is a gap today.

1. **A live inventory with dates.** Every item: where it is, when you bought it, when it expires. Receipts feed it, and meals take from it.
2. **A thaw plan.** A night-before reminder when tomorrow's dinner is frozen. Fridge thaw takes about 24 hours. Cold water takes 1 hour for flat-frozen thighs.
3. **A freezer log.** What went in and when. "Pink meat in clear wrap" never happens again.
4. **A leftovers log.** Cooked food is good for 3 to 4 days, then it's tomorrow's lunch or the trash.
5. **Staples with minimum amounts.** When rice cups, egg whites or Nurri drop below a set count, they go on the list on their own.
6. **A recipe book you rate.** Two plate sizes, macros for your plate, and what you thought after.
7. **A weekly plan and a prep plan.** Which dinners, what to batch on Sunday, what to pull from the freezer and when.
8. **Danielle's side.** Her preferences, her frozen meals, and her plate for every shared dinner.
9. **Cost per week and per gram of protein,** by store.
10. **Food safety numbers:** chicken 165°F, ground beef 160°F, fish 145°F. This needs a thermometer.
